2b · a song with no lyrics found — the black dot

The glow carries its own legibility: a soft dark pocket under the words, so a line stays readable over busy artwork. That pocket is drawn by the words' own block — but it was drawn unconditionally, while the words themselves were not. On a song whose lyrics had not been found the block was therefore nothing but its own padding, a few dp of nothing, and the pocket still painted around that: a black dot on the cover with no words under it (left). The block is now only composed when it actually has a line to paint, so the pocket cannot exist without words (right).

4 · The list view itself — soft ghost tiles, everywhere

The card fill sat under every row in the app — playlists, albums, search, library, notifications, the Spotify tabs — so a list of ten songs read as ten buttons, each with its own border and its own rounded box, and the ⋮ lived in a fenced-off column. Rows are now ghost tiles: at rest they paint nothing at all and the list reads as a list. The tonal fill is kept for the rows that earned one — the playing row gets a 10 % wash, rows picked in selection mode get 18 % — and it fades in rather than snapping. Artwork corners tightened to 12 dp (the grid keeps its 20 dp), tile corners to 16 dp, ⋮ flush right with no divider. One component paints every list, so this is every list.

Same look from the screenshot, everywhere in the app: surface-filled pills, 18–20 dp corners, fenced ⋮.

After — ghost tiles rebuilt

5 · Mini player mesh out · Spotify lyrics in

Two changes to settings. The mini player's background style drops Animated Mesh — the drifting gradient blobs were the least-loved option and the glow style already does soft colour behind the bar (the full player keeps its own mesh choice). And lyrics get a real priority editor: every provider in one list, a switch on each row, and arrows to move a provider up or down — the highest-ranked provider that has lyrics now wins, even when a lower one answers faster. New to the list: Spotify lyrics — it asks the account you already signed in with, and every lyric it fetches is saved into the app for offline playback.

Switching one off keeps its place (dimmed) instead of shuffling the list; every arrow tap saves instantly. Drag-to-reorder is gone — arrows are the reorder.

Also in this build — the canvas that stayed dark. Leaving the app in the background and coming back used to leave the animated canvas dead for the rest of the track: stopping the app releases the window the video was drawing into, so the decoder errored, the three retries were spent on a screen nobody could see, and the player came back holding a track that had already given up. The canvas now follows the app — it pauses while the app is stopped, spends no retries there, and on the way back it is given a fresh video surface, a fresh retry budget and a re-prepare, so it fades back in.

Mini lyrics themselves are unchanged by default. The new style is opt-in and works in every player design — Capsule, Cinematic, Vinyl, Apple Music, Classic, Modern, Wavy and Vivi — each drawing the line low on its own artwork. Whichever style is chosen is the only one drawn, so the strip and the glow never appear together.

And the line lands on the word now. A player publishes its position once per audio buffer, so between publications the number the mini lyrics were reading simply did not move — every line changed a beat after it was sung. The clock is now re-extrapolated from the last published position with wall time, exactly as the full lyrics view has always done, so the two agree instead of drifting apart by up to a buffer.

Spotify lyrics, and how priority now bites. The Spotify provider resolves the playing song with the same track search the animated canvas uses — so lyrics and canvas can never disagree about which Spotify track this is — then pulls synced lines from Spotify's own lyrics endpoint and writes an LRC copy into app storage, served from there on every later play, offline. And inside the race the winner is picked by rank: the top provider answering with timed lyrics settles it immediately; a lower-ranked answer is only locked in after a short window during which nobody above it produced timed lyrics. Moving a provider up actually means something now.

And the list rebuild. The base row every list in the app is built from (songs, albums, playlists, artists, YouTube results, notifications — ~90 call sites) no longer paints a card behind each row. The playing and selected rows keep a tonal wash so they still stand out; the recognition-history row, the last list that drew its own cards, was converted too.
