Sea glass surfaces — mini player bar & login pills

Two places in Glossy now render optically modelled glass instead of a flat themed fill: a fifth mini player style (Sea Glass) and the two action pills on the login card. Both use the vendored SeaGlass library (third_party/SeaGlass), wired in as :seaglass-core and :seaglass-compose.

This page is a CSS approximation, not the real render. On device the surface is drawn by the SeaGlass AGSL shader on the GPU — Beer–Lambert body absorption, sand-frosted micro-facets, and rim refraction with chromatic dispersion. What is accurate here is the geometry and the material numbers taken straight from the Kotlin; the optics are approximated with backdrop-filter, grain and inset rims. Do not judge shader performance from this page.
1The Sea Glass mini player

The bar has no access to the screen behind it, so it carries its own stage: the artwork is drawn once into a GPU layer and the bar's body refracts that layer. The strip below is that captured layer — the bar reads it, not the panel background.

Appearance → Mini player → Sea Glass (experimental) selects it. Nothing captures a layer while any other style is active.

Pale, see-through bodies with dark ink: the glass is a pastel surface, not a colour fill, and the shadow under each pill is tinted with that pill's own ink so the lift never greys the white card.

Tooling previews have no GPU pass to sample, so under LocalInspectionMode the pills fall back to the flat fill and the plain backdrop — that is why fill is still passed alongside the material.

3The playback queue

The queue's header is now the design from the reference: Playback Queue with a collapse chevron, then the track count and Playing next on one line with an Autoplay switch and a search button. Searching filters the list in place.

Rows trade the overflow menu for a direct ✕ beside the drag handle. The ✕ and the swipe run one remove path — undo snackbar included — so the two can never drift apart. While a search is on, the drag handle is held back: a drag would move items by their position in the filtered list, which no longer matches the queue.

4What to look at on device
  • Contrast: does the title on the bar survive a bright, busy cover? The bar's tint is deep (density 0.62) — if it reads grey rather than glass, the frost is doing too little.
  • Rim: chalkiness 0.72 on the bar and 0.74 on the guest pill is deliberate. Look for a hairline catching light at the top edge, not a white outline.
  • Login pills: the white card behind them is the one bright surface on that screen — check the glass does not turn the pills milky, and that the Google pill still reads as brand violet.
  • Cost: the mini player records its own GPU layer only while this style is selected.
